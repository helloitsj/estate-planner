First, situations that belong with an attorney from the start

If any of these apply, that's the tool pointing you to the right conversation, not a dead end: a business or professional practice, real estate in another state, a child or beneficiary with a disability or special needs, a combined estate large enough to owe estate tax, or — for a couple — a spouse who isn't a U.S. citizen. Each is a set of questions for an attorney.

Otherwise, a few questions

  • Decision-makers during incapacity — have you (and a co-parent, if any) named an agent, and a backup, for medical and financial decisions? See why a power of attorney and a health care proxy end at death.
  • Guardianship — who raises your children if you're gone, and, separately, who steps in if a parent is alive but unable to care for them? For a single parent, whether the child's other parent is involved matters too. See the incapacity gap and SCPA 1726.
  • Children's money — outright at set ages or through a trust, and whether the person who raises them also controls the money. See the both-parents-gone state on the timeline.

Each ends in questions to ask your attorney, plus facts worth confirming — never a verdict on your plan.
