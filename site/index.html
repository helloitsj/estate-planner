Who decides what, and when

Everything written about estate planning is organized by document. That's the wrong index. Nobody wonders what a power of attorney is — they wonder who signs for the apartment while they're in a coma, and whether that changes the moment they die.

Educational information, not legal advice. This site describes how estate-planning instruments generally operate in a specific state. It does not account for any individual situation, and law varies by state and changes over time. Nothing here creates an attorney–client relationship. Consult a licensed attorney in your state.

Why this framing

A power of attorney terminates at death. A will's guardian operates at death, not during incapacity. These are mechanisms — true across the arc of a life, and largely true across state lines. This site leads with those durable mechanics and, where a state's nuance takes over, hands it to you as a question for a licensed attorney rather than a conclusion.
